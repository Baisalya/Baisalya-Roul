# EduSheet Marketing Strategy

## Positioning

**EduSheet is the math-first, offline-first question paper workspace for teachers.**

Lead with the problem it solves: typing formulas and building a clean paper should not require a general-purpose office editor or LaTeX knowledge. The strongest proof points are inline Math Keyboard, reusable Question Bank content, Geometry, OCR capture, OMR generation, and PDF/Word export.

Primary promise: **Type maths. Build papers. Teach more.**

## Priority audiences

1. **School teachers** — need syllabus-based papers, marks/sections, instructions and print-ready output.
2. **Private tutors** — need reusable chapter questions and fast worksheet/test variations.
3. **Coaching centres and institutions** — need repeatable paper styles, OMR sheets and a larger Windows workflow.

Start with teachers and tutors in India who prepare maths, science or mixed-subject assessments. Keep the message practical and bilingual: English for discovery, Hinglish/Hindi for trust and conversion.

## Conversion funnel

1. **Discover:** short formula-typing demos, before/after paper-creation posts and searchable guides.
2. **Understand:** send visitors to the Math Keyboard page or the 4-step Quick Start, not a generic homepage only.
3. **Try:** make the first task concrete—create one question, add one formula, preview and export.
4. **Return:** publish chapter-wise templates, Question Bank tips and exam-season reminders.
5. **Recommend:** ask teachers to share a generated sample paper or refer another teacher.

Every campaign should use one measurable landing URL, for example `?utm_source=youtube&utm_campaign=math-keyboard-demo`. Do not add behavioural analytics until the privacy notice and consent approach are ready.

## Channel plan

### Organic content

- **YouTube/Instagram Reels:** 20–40 second demos: fraction, integral, matrix, geometry figure, OCR-to-edit and OMR sheet.
- **YouTube long-form:** “Create a board-style maths paper in 5 minutes” and “Math Keyboard without LaTeX”.
- **Teacher communities:** share a useful sample paper or workflow tip first; put the download link after the value, not before it.
- **SEO pages:** target intent phrases such as `math question paper maker`, `equation typing for teachers`, `question paper maker offline`, `OMR sheet generator`, `Hindi maths paper maker` and `PDF Word question paper export`.

### Partnerships

- Maths tutors, coaching centres and teacher-training creators.
- School coordinators who prepare internal exams.
- Education YouTubers who can demonstrate a real paper rather than read a feature list.

Offer a co-branded sample paper or a guided workflow session. Avoid promising cloud collaboration, AI generation or backup until those capabilities are actually released.

### Paid acquisition

Windows is now publicly released on Microsoft Store; Android is in Google Play closed testing. Start with Windows conversion campaigns and use Android content primarily to recruit enrolled testers. Test three messages:

- “Type maths without LaTeX.”
- “Build a complete paper from one teacher workspace.”
- “Reuse questions and export PDF + Word.”

Measure install/download click, Quick Start completion, store conversion and cost per qualified teacher—not impressions alone. Pause any ad that attracts students looking for solved answers instead of paper authors.

## 30-day launch rhythm

### Week 1 — Proof and release clarity

Publish the Math Keyboard demo, Quick Start link, one sample paper and one clear privacy/offline post. Send Windows users to the live Microsoft Store listing and send eligible Android testers to the closed-test Google Play listing.

### Week 2 — Use cases

Publish separate examples for a school teacher, private tutor and coaching centre. Link each post to the relevant section on the homepage.

### Week 3 — Utility

Show Question Bank reuse, OCR proofreading, Geometry and OMR. Invite 5–10 teachers to attempt one real paper and report friction.

### Week 4 — Trust and referral

Publish a teacher workflow case study, a PDF/Word export demo and a referral prompt. Turn recurring questions into manual/FAQ content.

## Website rules

- Keep the first CTA focused on **Download** and the second on **See the Math Keyboard**.
- Keep feature claims aligned with the current app; label future settings such as cloud backup or AI model download as coming soon.
- Keep Android and Windows availability/status explicit. Never leave visitors guessing whether a disabled store button is a product issue.
- Preserve the existing bilingual switch, Quick Start, searchable Manual, Privacy page and direct support links.
- Use the homepage audience switcher and feature filters as discovery aids, not as substitutes for a real app demo.

## Ads and revenue guardrails

The existing ad part is intentionally preserved. Keep the current single responsive manual placement and centralized loader. It should remain:

- consent-gated;
- inactive until a real AdSense client, slot and consent-ready setup exist;
- limited to useful content pages;
- separate from the primary download CTA;
- absent from 404 and privacy/legal surfaces.

Do not add Auto Ads, pop-ups, interstitials or ad requests inside the app workflow. Product downloads, teacher trust and useful content come first; restrained website advertising is secondary revenue.

## Weekly scorecard

Track these numbers in a simple sheet:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Math Keyboard page visits | Measures USP interest |
| Quick Start completion | Measures activation intent |
| Android/Windows CTA clicks | Measures conversion intent |
| Qualified teacher installs | Measures audience quality |
| First paper/export completion | Measures product value |
| Returning visitors | Measures usefulness of content |
| Support questions by topic | Shows what the product or docs must clarify |

Review weekly, keep the best-performing message, and change one variable at a time.

## Immediate checklist

- Keep the verified Google Play closed-test and Microsoft Store release URLs in `EduSheet/assets/js/site.js`.
- Treat Windows as the public acquisition CTA; explain Android tester enrollment before asking for an install.
- Create one real sample paper for each priority audience.
- Record the Math Keyboard demo in English and Hinglish.
- Publish the Quick Start and Manual links with each campaign.
- Keep ads OFF until AdSense approval and consent readiness are confirmed.
- Ask early teachers for one sentence of feedback and one permissioned testimonial.
